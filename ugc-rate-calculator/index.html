UGC Rate Calculator

UGC rates are built from a package, not a follower count: a base price per finished video, plus extra hook variations, raw footage and however many months the brand may run the content as paid ads. The calculator above totals those lines from your own prices and shows a per-video figure.

UGC vs influencer pricing

UGC and influencer sponsorship are two different products, and confusing them is the fastest way to underprice. In a sponsorship deal the brand is buying access to your audience, so views drive the price. In a UGC deal the brand is buying content to run on its own channels, so your audience size is mostly irrelevant and your production output is what is being paid for.

The practical consequence: as a UGC creator you can quote from day one with no audience at all, and you should price by the package. If a deal involves both (you make the videos and also post them), quote the two parts separately and use the sponsorship rate calculator for the posting half.

Package add-ons

Most of the money in a UGC quote sits in the add-ons, which is why a flat per-video price loses out on scope creep. Price each one as a visible line.

LineWhat it coversHow to price it
Base videoScript, filming, edit, one revision roundYour hourly rate × realistic hours
Hook variationsAlternative openings for ad testingFlat price per extra hook, per video
Raw footageUnedited files the brand can recutFlat fee for the package
Paid usageRight to run the video as an adPercentage of the base, per month
Extra revisionsRounds beyond the one includedFlat price per round

Hook variations look small and are not. Brands testing creative want three or four openings per concept, and each one is another setup, take and export. Raw footage matters more still: once a brand holds your raw files it can produce cuts you never approved, indefinitely, so price it as a licence rather than a convenience.

Whether whitelisting or paid amplification is in scope should be settled before filming, and the FTC disclosure guidance for social media influencers guidance on disclosing a material connection applies to anything that runs under your own name.

Rates for beginners

Starting out, the difficulty is not knowing a market rate: it is that no reliable public one exists. Build your own floor instead, from numbers you can defend.

Time the work honestly for one video, start to finish: concept and script, setup, filming, editing, exporting, and the back-and-forth over email. If that is four hours and you would not get out of bed for less than a given hourly figure, multiply. That product is your base price per video, and it is the only number on the page you should never go below, because it is the one that determines whether this is worth doing at all.

Then add the package lines: extra hooks, raw footage, usage months. Quote a small package (for example three videos with two extra hooks each) rather than a single video, since the setup cost is the same and the brand gets more to test.

Two things to avoid early on. Do not accept free product as payment for usage rights, which are what the brand actually wants. And do not sign perpetual, all-channel usage into a first contract at a first-contract price, because you cannot reprice it later.

Frequently asked questions

What are the expected UGC rates in 2026?

No figure is published here, and it is worth being suspicious of sites that do publish one without naming a dated survey behind it. Rates vary by category, deliverable count and usage terms, and calculator pages tend to copy each other's numbers. Price from your own cost base and the rights being licensed, then let the market answer by how quickly briefs are accepted.

How much should I get paid for UGC?

At minimum, your time for the whole job plus a separate fee for whatever rights the brand takes. Count scripting, setup, filming, editing and revisions, apply a rate you would accept for freelance work, and treat that as the floor per video. Add hooks, raw footage and usage months on top rather than absorbing them into one price.

How much does an UGC cost?

For a brand, the cost is a function of scope: how many videos, how many hook variations, whether raw files are included, how many revision rounds, and how long the content may run as paid media. Each of those should appear as a line in the quote. The calculator above builds that total so both sides can see which line to change when the budget is tight.

How to charge for UGC content?

Quote packages with itemised lines: base price per video, per-hook price for extra openings, a flat fee for raw footage, and a monthly usage fee as a percentage of the base. Put the usage term in months, state the number of revision rounds included, and invoice with clear payment terms. Itemising is what lets a brand cut scope instead of cutting your rate.
